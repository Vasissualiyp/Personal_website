# Understanding Population III Stars

Population III stars are the first generation of stars that formed in the universe, roughly 100-200 million years after the Big Bang. Unlike modern stars, they formed from pristine gas containing only hydrogen and helium, with no heavier elements.

## Why Are They Important?

Pop III stars are crucial to understanding:

- **Reionization**: They were the first sources of light that reionized the universe
- **Element Formation**: Through supernovae, they created the first heavy elements
- **Black Hole Seeds**: Some may have collapsed directly into intermediate-mass black holes

## Simulation Challenges

Simulating Pop III star formation is computationally demanding because:

1. **High Resolution Requirements**: We need to resolve scales from cosmological (Mpc) down to stellar (AU)
2. **Complex Physics**: Cooling, chemistry, radiative transfer all matter
3. **Long Timescales**: Formation takes millions of years in simulation time

## Current Work at CITA

At CITA, we're using the GIZMO code to run high-resolution simulations of Pop III star formation. Our goal is to determine the Initial Mass Function (IMF) - the distribution of stellar masses at birth.

Early results suggest Pop III stars may have been much more massive than typical stars today, with masses potentially ranging from 10 to 1000 solar masses.

## Next Steps

We're working on:
- Incorporating more realistic magnetic fields
- Modeling stellar feedback and radiative effects  
- Extending simulations to track multiple star formation episodes
